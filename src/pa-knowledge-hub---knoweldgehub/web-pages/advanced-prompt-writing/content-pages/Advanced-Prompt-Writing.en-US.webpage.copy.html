Advanced Prompt Writing

Basic prompts tell Copilot what you want. Advanced prompts give Copilot a complete brief, helping it deliver richer insights, better analysis and more relevant outputs.

What Makes A Prompt Advanced?

Advanced prompting goes beyond simply asking Copilot to complete a task. It provides clear objectives, business context, audience information, reference materials and expected outputs.

Think of it as creating a project brief rather than asking a simple question.

Why Use Advanced Prompts?

Better Analysis

More context allows Copilot to provide deeper insights rather than generic responses.

More Relevant Content

Defining the audience and objective helps ensure the output is suitable for the people receiving it.

Higher Quality Outputs

Clear structure and requirements generally produce stronger and more consistent results.

The R.O.C.K.S Framework

Advanced prompts work best when you build them layer by layer. Start with the role, define the objective, provide context, supply knowledge and finish by defining the structure of the output.

R

Role

Tell Copilot who it should act as.

Act as an experienced Operational Reporting Manager.
O

Objective

Define the outcome you are trying to achieve.

Create an executive-level performance report.
C

Context

Explain the business background and purpose of the task.

This report will be reviewed during a monthly leadership meeting.
K

Knowledge

Provide supporting information, reports and documents.

Analyse the attached performance reports and supporting conversion data.
S

Structure

Define how the final output should be organised.

Include an Executive Summary, Key Trends, Risks, Opportunities and Recommended Actions.

Basic Prompt Vs Advanced Prompt

Basic Prompt

Create a presentation about performance.

Copilot knows very little about the objective, audience or desired outcome.

Advanced Prompt

Act as an experienced Operational Reporting Manager. Analyse the attached performance data and create an executive-level PowerPoint presentation for the Senior Leadership Team. Include an Executive Summary, Key Trends, Notable Changes, Risks, Opportunities and Recommended Actions. Focus on insights and recommendations rather than simply repeating the data. Keep the tone professional and concise and limit the presentation to four slides with speaker notes.

This prompt provides a clear role, objective, audience, structure and expected outcome.
